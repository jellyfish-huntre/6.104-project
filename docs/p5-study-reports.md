# P5 study reports

## Participant 1

Participant 1 is a student who might attend a club or career fair. He entered Demo Fair with its code and did not try to make an account. While exploring, he clicked tables and walked into them. Then he asked, “what do I do at booths?” He later told me he thought Roam was a game where he interacted with the booth itself. I first told him to keep exploring, then explained that Roam is an online club or career fair. I did not point out controls or give task hints.

I think the empty fair was part of his confusion. With no other visitors at the booths, the tables may have looked like the things to interact with. That may be partly a problem with how I set up the study, but a real fair could also be quiet. I cannot tell from this session whether more visible people, clearer booth descriptions, or a brief introduction would have helped most.

After I explained the premise, he completed the rest of the tasks without hesitation or help. He recognized that Campus Garden Lab had no live conversation, joined an open call, requested a turn, found the private link after the reload and invitation, and left the fair. This shows he could use the visitor flow once he knew what the fair was for. It does not show he would have figured that out alone. He said afterward that he liked the idea. He never opened or commented on the directory, so I cannot say he had trouble finding it. He may simply have preferred walking around.

## Participant 2

Participant 2 is a student who might attend a club or career fair. She entered with the fair code and did not try to make an account. Walking into booths brought up descriptions, and those popups became her main way of learning about the groups. She liked the outline showing her current booth and the option to minimize its description. During the first exploration task, she saw “Request a turn” in a booth description and clicked it before the queue task. She understood that she had joined a queue. The button caught her attention even though I think its wording could be clearer. She completed the other tasks without hints.

She noticed the directory afterward and said its icon was not very visible or obvious to click. She suggested moving it to the upper left and making it stand out more. She also said walking around with the avatar felt natural. Her route leaves me with two possible explanations: the directory was too subtle, or walking and reading popups already met her needs. I would test this with a task that asks someone to find a specific booth.

After the reload, I sent her invitation. She noticed it, read it, and opened the private meeting link. When I asked about possible improvements afterward, she suggested sprinting or jumping directly to a booth from the directory because crossing a larger map could feel slow. This was a prediction about a larger map, not something that stopped her in this six-booth fair. I would test a longer route before changing movement, since walking was also how she found and learned about the booths.
