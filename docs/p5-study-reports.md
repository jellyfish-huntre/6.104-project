# P5 study reports

## Participant 1

Participant 1 is a student who might attend a club or career fair. He entered Demo Fair with its code and did not try to make an account. While exploring, he tried clicking tables and walking into them. He asked, “what do I do at booths?” He later told me he thought Roam was a game where he interacted with the booth itself. I told him to keep exploring and explained Roam as an online club or career fair. I did not point out controls or give task hints.

I think the empty fair was part of why he was confused. There were no other visitors at the booths, so it was less clear that the people, rather than the objects, were what he was meant to interact with. That may have been caused partly by how I set up the study, but a real fair could also be quiet. I cannot tell from this session whether showing more people, making the booth descriptions clearer, or giving a brief introduction would have helped most.

After I explained the premise, he completed the rest of the tasks without hesitation or help. He recognized that Campus Garden Lab had no live conversation, joined an open call, requested a turn, found the private link after a reload, and left the fair. This tells me the main visitor flow worked for him once he understood the concept, but it does not tell me whether he would have figured it out on his own. He said afterward that he liked the idea. He never opened or commented on the directory, so I cannot say he had trouble finding it. He may simply have preferred walking around.

## Participant 2

Participant 2 is a student who might attend a club or career fair. She entered with the fair code and did not try to make an account. She explored by walking around and reading descriptions that appeared when she entered booths. She liked the outline showing her current booth and the option to minimize its description. During the first exploration task, she saw the “request a turn” button in a booth description and clicked it before the queue task. She understood that she had joined a queue. This suggests the button was easy to notice, even though its wording could be clearer. She completed the other tasks without hints.

She noticed the directory afterward and said the icon was not very visible or obvious to click. She suggested moving it to the upper left and making the icon stand out more. At the same time, she said walking around with the avatar felt natural. I think both things could be true: the directory could be easier to see, but she also had little reason to use it because walking and reading the popups worked for her. I would test the directory again with a task that asks someone to find a specific booth.

After reloading, she noticed her invitation, read it, and opened the private meeting link. When I asked about possible improvements afterward, she suggested sprinting or jumping directly to a booth from the directory because crossing a larger map could feel slow. This was a suggestion about a larger map, not something that stopped her during the study. I would try a longer route with another participant before changing movement, since walking was also how she found and learned about the booths.
