# P5 user study tasks

Roam is meant to help someone find a conversation they did not schedule in advance, then keep exploring if the person they want to meet is busy. I will use the same tasks with two participants. Each session reserves about 40 minutes for tasks and 20 minutes for a debrief. The times below are guides.

The participant opens [Roam](https://roam.mit-sdg.dev/) in a fresh guest session with the Demo Fair code `3BC7D6B4`. The six booths and the representative-controlled call states are described in the [study setup](p5-study-setup.md). I will ask the participant to think aloud, let them try to recover from difficulties, and record any help I give.

| Task                                         | Instruction to the participant                                                                                                                            | Why I included it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Arrive at the fair (3 min)                | “You're invited to the Demo Fair. Go ahead and see which groups are here.”                                                                                | This starts with a simple goal and tests whether the code, guest entry, and first view give a newcomer enough direction. Hopefully watching someone else shows which part they notice first and whether they think an account is required.                                                                                                   |
| 2. Choose where to start (6 min)             | “You are interested in making local transit easier to use. Find two groups whose work might fit, then decide which one you would approach first and why.” | The fair has both Open Maps Collective and Civic Data Studio, so the choice depends on reading and comparing real descriptions. I want to see whether the directory and map help a visitor form a plan, rather than just stumbling across them on the open world.                                                                            |
| 3. Check an unavailable conversation (5 min) | "Go to the Campus Garden Lab booth and see if you can speak with someone there right now.”                                                                | This booth has no active call. The task tests whether a visitor distinguishes a booth worth exploring from a live invitation to talk. Their explanation should reveal whether an inactive booth looks unavailable, broken, or simply quiet.                                                                                                  |
| 4. Join an open conversation (8 min)         | "You have a question for Lantern Robotics, ask someone whether beginners can join Thursday build nights.”                                                 | This crosses from discovering a booth to entering its area and opening an external meeting. I want to observe where the participant expects the conversation to begin, whether the live state is clear, and what they make of the move from Roam to the meeting tab.                                                                         |
| 5. Wait without standing still (9 min)       | “Lantern Robotics is now speaking with someone else, but you still want a short one-to-one conversation. Join the queue, then go explore another booth.”  | This is Roam's main alternative to staying in a crowded video room. The sequence tests whether requesting a turn feels separate from entering a call, and whether the participant trusts that their place remains while they walk away. A walkthrough can confirm the queue persists, but it cannot show whether a visitor believes it will. |
| 6. Return for the conversation (7 min)       | “Your fair page has reloaded while you were waiting. Find out whether you still have a turn and, if Lantern Robotics is ready for you, speak with them.”  | Reloading tests whether the request and invitation still make sense without relying on the participant's memory of a previous screen. I will watch how they notice the invitation, interpret its time limit, and recover the private meeting link.                                                                                           |
| 7. Finish the visit (2 min)                  | “You are done for now. Leave the fair.”                                                                                                                   | This ends the visit naturally and checks whether leaving the fair is distinct from closing a meeting tab or a booth panel. It also gives the participant a chance to say whether they think their presence or call access remains afterward.                                                                                                 |

## Facilitator transitions

Before each session, I will verify the [Demo Fair setup](p5-study-setup.md): six booths are visible, Lantern Robotics has an open group call, and the participant has no earlier request. I will be present as the Lantern Robotics representative in the study-controlled meeting, so task 4 can involve a real conversation. After task 4, I will switch that booth to invite-only mode with the separate private meeting link. This is a planned state change, not an action the participant must discover. I will describe the situation in task 5 without naming Roam's controls.

After the participant requests a turn and has spent about one minute exploring another booth, I will send an invitation from the representative session. At the start of task 6, I will reload the participant's fair page to create the stated situation. I will use the same timing and sequence in both studies, and note any deviation. If a participant cannot complete a task, I will record where they stopped and any intervention before moving on; the next task should not be reported as independent success if I had to set it up for them.

The final 20 minutes are for questions about moments I observed: what the participant expected, what they tried, and how they interpreted Roam's response. I will keep observations separate from my explanations when writing the two study reports.
